Gas Problem Set: Ch 8 (8.1–8.5), 27 problems from easy to exam level

Work on scratch paper or in the spaces. Answers and full worked solutions are in gas_problems_key.pdf. ★ = exam-level problem. Use R = 0.08206 L·atm/(mol·K) unless told otherwise.

THE 5-STEP GAS ROUTINE (do this for EVERY gas problem)
  1. Make a table of P, V, n, T. Write each given value with its unit and put "?" for the one you want. Two states (before/after)? Make two columns.
  2. Convert: T → K (+273.15) · P → atm (÷760 for mmHg/Torr, ÷101.325 for kPa) · V → L (mL ÷ 1000) · grams → mol (÷𝓜).
  3. Pick the equation:
    SituationUse
    One state, find one of P, V, n, TPV = nRT
    Same gas, conditions change (before → after)P₁V₁/(n₁T₁) = P₂V₂/(n₂T₂); cross out what stays constant
    Grams + volume given, find 𝓜 or density𝓜 = mRT/(PV) · d = P𝓜/(RT)
    Mixture of gasesP_tot = ΣP_i · P_i = χ_i·P_tot · P_i = n_iRT/V
    Collected over waterP_gas = P_total − P_H₂O
    Reaction involving a gasgas ⇄ mol via PV = nRT, then mole ratio. Same T and P? volume ratio = mole ratio
    Rates / effusionrate₁/rate₂ = √(𝓜₂/𝓜₁) · time₁/time₂ = √(𝓜₁/𝓜₂)
    Speeds / kinetic energyu_rms = √(3RT/𝓜), R = 8.314, 𝓜 in kg/mol · KE_avg = 3/2 RT (per mol)
  4. Predict first: does the answer go up or down? (e.g. hotter → bigger V; more pressure → smaller V)
  5. Solve, then check units, sig figs, and that the direction matches your prediction.

A. Pressure units & the simple gas laws

1. Convert 0.875 atm into mm-Hg, kPa, and bar. Then convert a pressure of 642 mm-Hg into atm.

2. (Boyle) A sealed syringe holds 50.0 mL of air at 1.00 atm. The plunger is pushed in until the volume is 12.5 mL at constant temperature. What is the new pressure?

3. (Charles) A 2.50-L balloon at 22.0 °C is dipped into liquid nitrogen (−196 °C) at constant pressure. What is its new volume?

4. (Gay-Lussac) A car tire is at 32.0 psi at 15 °C in the morning. After driving, the tire is at 45 °C. Assuming constant volume, what is the new pressure (treat psi as absolute pressure)?

5. ★ (Combined) A weather balloon holds 5.00 L of He at 755 mm-Hg and 25 °C at launch. It rises to an altitude where P = 380. mm-Hg and T = −15 °C. What is its volume there? Explain why it expanded even though it got colder.

6. (Avogadro) A 3.00-L container holds 0.125 mol of gas. If 0.0500 mol more gas is added at constant T and P, what will the new volume be?

B. Ideal gas law, PV = nRT

7. A 15.0-L scuba tank contains O₂ at 150. atm and 22 °C. How many moles and how many kilograms of O₂ does it hold?

8. 4.50 g of CO₂ is placed in a 2.00-L flask at body temperature (37 °C). What is the pressure in atm?

9. At what temperature (in K and °C) will 1.00 mol of a gas in a 22.4-L container exert a pressure of 2.00 atm?

C. Gas density & molar mass

10. Calculate the density of Cl₂ gas (70.90 g/mol) at STP and at 100. °C and 1.00 atm. Why does the density go down?

11. ★ A hydrocarbon contains 85.6% C and 14.4% H by mass. A 0.802-g sample of its vapor occupies 354 mL at 100. °C and 752 mm-Hg. Determine the molar mass and the molecular formula.

12. (Concept) At the same T and P, rank He, air (𝓜 ≈ 29 g/mol), and CO₂ by density. Which balloons float in air and which sink?

D. Dalton's law & collecting gas over water

13. ★ A 5.00-L flask at 25 °C contains 2.00 g He and 8.00 g O₂. Find the partial pressure of each gas, the total pressure, and the mole fraction of He.

14. Dry air is 20.95 mol-% O₂. On a day when the barometric pressure is 0.950 atm, what is the partial pressure of O₂ in atm and in mm-Hg?

15. ★ Heating KClO₃ gives O₂: 2 KClO₃(s) → 2 KCl(s) + 3 O₂(g). The O₂ is collected over water: 0.512 L at 22 °C and a total pressure of 758 mm-Hg (vapor pressure of water at 22 °C = 19.8 mm-Hg). What mass of KClO₃ (122.55 g/mol) decomposed?

E. Gas stoichiometry

16. ★ Car airbags inflate by 2 NaN₃(s) → 2 Na(s) + 3 N₂(g). What mass of NaN₃ (65.01 g/mol) is needed to fill a 65.0-L airbag at 1.10 atm and 25 °C?

17. 15.0 L H₂ and 6.00 L N₂ react at the same T and P: N₂(g) + 3 H₂(g) → 2 NH₃(g). Which is limiting? What volume of NH₃ forms, and what volume of which gas is left over? (No ideal gas law needed!)

18. 10.0 g of propane (C₃H₈, 44.097 g/mol) burns: C₃H₈ + 5 O₂ → 3 CO₂ + 4 H₂O. (a) What volume of CO₂ forms at STP? (b) What volume of O₂ at 25 °C and 1.00 atm is consumed?

19. ★ 2.00 g of Al (26.982 g/mol) is reacted with 1.50 L of Cl₂ gas at 25 °C and 1.00 atm: 2 Al(s) + 3 Cl₂(g) → 2 AlCl₃(s). Determine the limiting reactant and the mass of AlCl₃ (133.33 g/mol) formed.

F. Effusion (Graham) & kinetic-molecular theory

20. (a) How many times faster does H₂ effuse than O₂? (b) An unknown gas effuses 0.354 times as fast as He. What is its molar mass, and what could it be? (c) 1.00 L of N₂ effuses through a pinhole in 105 s; the same volume of an unknown gas takes 191 s. Find the unknown's molar mass.

21. ★ Cotton balls soaked in NH₃(aq) and HCl(aq) are placed at opposite ends of a 1.00-m glass tube at the same time. A white ring of NH₄Cl(s) forms where the gases meet. How far from the NH₃ end does the ring form?

22. (a) Calculate u_rms for He and for Xe (131.29 g/mol) at 25 °C. (b) At what temperature would O₂ molecules have the same u_rms as He atoms at 25 °C?

23. Calculate the average kinetic energy of a mole of gas molecules and of a single molecule at 25 °C. Does it matter which gas?

24. (Concept) For a fixed amount of ideal gas, what happens to P, average KE, and u_rms when (a) T is doubled (in K) at constant V; (b) V is halved at constant T? Explain each with KMT.

G. Barometers & real gases

25. (a) How tall would a water barometer be at 1.00 atm (ρ(Hg) = 13.56 g/mL, ρ(H₂O) = 1.00 g/mL)? (b) What pressure (in atm) does a 10.0-m column of water exert (P = ρgh, g = 9.80665 m/s²)?

26. (Concept) Which deviates more from ideal behavior and why: (a) He or NH₃ at 1 atm; (b) N₂ at 1 atm and 300 K or at 300 atm and 150 K? What are the two ideal-gas assumptions that fail, and what does Z = PV/(nRT) equal for an ideal gas?

H. Challenge

27. ★★ A rigid 2.00-L flask at 400. K contains 0.200 mol CO and 0.150 mol O₂. The reaction 2 CO(g) + O₂(g) → 2 CO₂(g) goes to completion at constant T. Find the total pressure before the reaction, the partial pressure of each gas after the reaction, and the total pressure after. Why did the pressure drop?
