Gas Problem Set: Worked Solutions

Each solution follows the 5-step routine: table → convert → pick equation → predict → solve/check. Red = common mistake. R = 0.08206 L·atm/(mol·K), STP = 273.15 K and 1 atm (22.414 L/mol).

A. Pressure units & simple gas laws

1. 0.875 atm × 760 = 665 mm-Hg; × 101.325 = 88.7 kPa; × 1.01325 = 0.887 bar. 642 mm-Hg ÷ 760 = 0.845 atm.
2. P₁=1.00 atm V₁=50.0 mL | P₂=? V₂=12.5 mL; n and T constant → P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ → P₂ = 1.00 × 50.0/12.5 = 4.00 atm. Prediction: volume ÷4 → pressure ×4 ✓. (mL is fine here because the units cancel.)
3. T₁ = 295.15 K, T₂ = 77.15 K. V₂ = V₁(T₂/T₁) = 2.50 × 77.15/295.15 = 0.653 L. Using °C gives a negative volume, which is impossible. Always use K!
4. P₂ = P₁(T₂/T₁) = 32.0 × 318.15/288.15 = 35.3 psi. Any pressure unit works for ratios; only T must be in K.
5. P₁=755 mmHg V₁=5.00 L T₁=298.15 K | P₂=380. mmHg V₂=? T₂=258.15 K, n constant.
V₂ = V₁ × (P₁/P₂) × (T₂/T₁) = 5.00 × (755/380.) × (258.15/298.15) = 5.00 × 1.987 × 0.8658 = 8.60 L.
Pressure dropped by about half (×1.99 for V), but T dropped only 13% (×0.866). The pressure effect wins, so the balloon expands. Trick: write each change as a ratio factor and ask whether it should make V bigger or smaller.
6. V/n constant: V₂ = 3.00 L × (0.175/0.125) = 4.20 L. n₂ is the TOTAL (0.125 + 0.0500), not 0.0500.

B. PV = nRT

7. n = PV/RT = (150.)(15.0)/(0.08206 × 295.15) = 92.9 mol O₂ × 31.998 g/mol = 2973 g = 2.97 kg.
8. n = 4.50/44.009 = 0.1023 mol; T = 310.15 K. P = nRT/V = (0.1023)(0.08206)(310.15)/2.00 = 1.30 atm.
9. T = PV/(nR) = (2.00)(22.4)/(1.00 × 0.08206) = 546 K = 273 °C. Check: at STP 1 mol in 22.4 L is at 1 atm, so doubling P at constant V means doubling T (273 K → 546 K) ✓.

C. Density & molar mass

10. d = P𝓜/(RT): STP: (1.00)(70.90)/(0.08206 × 273.15) = 3.16 g/L; 100 °C: (1.00)(70.90)/(0.08206 × 373.15) = 2.32 g/L. Hotter gas → same mass spread over a larger volume → less dense (d ∝ 1/T).
11. Empirical: C 85.6/12.011 = 7.127, H 14.4/1.008 = 14.29 → 1 : 2.00 → CH₂ (14.027 g/mol).
m=0.802 g, V=0.354 L, T=373.15 K, P=752/760=0.9895 atm
𝓜 = mRT/(PV) = (0.802)(0.08206)(373.15)/[(0.9895)(0.354)] = 70.1 g/mol. k = 70.1/14.027 = 5.00 → C₅H₁₀.
Exam combo: % composition → empirical formula, gas data → 𝓜, then k = 𝓜/𝓜_emp.
12. At the same T and P, d ∝ 𝓜: He (4) < air (29) < CO₂ (44). He balloons float; CO₂-filled balloons sink.

D. Dalton & over water

13. n(He) = 2.00/4.0026 = 0.4997 mol; n(O₂) = 8.00/31.998 = 0.2500 mol. RT/V = (0.08206 × 298.15)/5.00 = 4.893 atm/mol.
P(He) = 0.4997 × 4.893 = 2.45 atm; P(O₂) = 0.2500 × 4.893 = 1.22 atm; P_tot = 3.67 atm. χ(He) = 0.4997/0.7497 = 0.667 (check: 0.667 × 3.67 = 2.45 ✓).
The partial pressure depends on MOLES, not grams: 8 g O₂ gives less pressure than 2 g He.
14. P(O₂) = χ·P_tot = 0.2095 × 0.950 = 0.199 atm = 151 mm-Hg.
15. P(O₂) = 758 − 19.8 = 738.2 mm-Hg = 0.9713 atm; T = 295.15 K; V = 0.512 L.
n(O₂) = (0.9713)(0.512)/(0.08206 × 295.15) = 0.02053 mol → × (2 KClO₃/3 O₂) = 0.01369 mol → × 122.55 = 1.68 g KClO₃.
Two traps: forgetting to subtract the water vapor, and forgetting the 2:3 ratio.

E. Gas stoichiometry

16. n(N₂) = PV/RT = (1.10)(65.0)/(0.08206 × 298.15) = 2.922 mol → × (2/3) = 1.948 mol NaN₃ × 65.01 = 127 g NaN₃.
Pattern: gas → mol via PV=nRT, then mole ratio, then grams.
17. Same T and P → liters behave like moles. 6.00 L N₂ would need 18.0 L H₂, but only 15.0 L is available → H₂ limiting. NH₃ = 15.0 × (2/3) = 10.0 L. N₂ used = 15.0/3 = 5.00 L → 1.00 L N₂ left over.
18. n(C₃H₈) = 10.0/44.097 = 0.2268 mol. (a) CO₂ = 3 × 0.2268 = 0.6803 mol × 22.414 L/mol = 15.2 L at STP. (b) O₂ = 5 × 0.2268 = 1.134 mol; V = nRT/P = (1.134)(0.08206)(298.15)/1.00 = 27.7 L.
22.414 L/mol is ONLY valid at STP (0 °C, 1 atm); at 25 °C use PV = nRT (24.47 L/mol).
19. n(Al) = 2.00/26.982 = 0.07412 mol (÷2 = 0.0371). n(Cl₂) = (1.00)(1.50)/(0.08206 × 298.15) = 0.06131 mol (÷3 = 0.0204) → Cl₂ is limiting.
AlCl₃ = 0.06131 × (2/3) = 0.04087 mol × 133.33 = 5.45 g AlCl₃.

F. Graham & KMT

20. (a) rate(H₂)/rate(O₂) = √(31.998/2.016) = 3.98× faster.
(b) rate(X)/rate(He) = √(𝓜_He/𝓜_X) → 0.354 = √(4.0026/𝓜) → 𝓜 = 4.0026/0.354² = 31.9 g/mol → O₂.
(c) Time is the inverse of rate: t_X/t_N₂ = √(𝓜_X/𝓜_N₂) → 𝓜 = 28.014 × (191/105)² = 92.7 g/mol.
Heavier gas = slower rate = LONGER time. Square both sides to solve for 𝓜.
21. rate(NH₃)/rate(HCl) = √(36.461/17.031) = 1.463. In the same time, the distances are in the same ratio: d(NH₃) = 1.463/(1 + 1.463) × 100 cm = 59.4 cm from the NH₃ end (the ring forms closer to the HCl end because HCl is heavier and slower).
22. u_rms = √(3RT/𝓜), R = 8.314 J/(mol·K), T = 298.15 K.
He: √(3 × 8.314 × 298.15/0.0040026) = 1.36 × 10³ m/s; Xe: √(3 × 8.314 × 298.15/0.13129) = 238 m/s.
(b) Equal speeds mean equal T/𝓜: T(O₂) = 298.15 × (31.998/4.0026) = 2.38 × 10³ K.
𝓜 in kg/mol (4.0026 g → 0.0040026 kg). With g/mol you'd get an answer √1000 ≈ 31.6× too small.
23. KE_avg = 3/2 RT = 1.5 × 8.314 × 298.15 = 3.72 kJ/mol; per molecule 3/2 k_B T = 6.17 × 10⁻²¹ J. Doesn't depend on the gas: KE depends only on T. Heavier gases have the same KE but move slower.
24. (a) T ×2, V fixed: P ×2, KE ×2, u_rms ×√2 (1.41). Faster molecules hit the walls harder and more often.
(b) V ÷2, T fixed: P ×2, KE and u_rms unchanged. Same speeds, but more molecules per wall area, so there are more collisions per second.

G. Barometers & real gases

25. (a) Height ∝ 1/ρ: 760 mm × 13.56/1.00 = 10 306 mm = 10.3 m. (b) P = ρgh = (1000 kg/m³)(9.80665 m/s²)(10.0 m) = 9.81 × 10⁴ Pa ÷ 101 325 = 0.968 atm.
26. (a) NH₃: it is polar and H-bonds (strong attractions) and is a larger molecule; He is the most ideal gas. (b) 300 atm, 150 K. The failed assumptions are (1) particles have no volume (fails at high P) and (2) no interparticle forces (fails at low T). Ideal gas: Z = 1.

H. Challenge

27. RT/V = (0.08206 × 400.)/2.00 = 16.41 atm/mol.
Before: 0.350 mol → P = 5.74 atm.
Reaction (mol): CO limits (0.200 CO needs only 0.100 O₂). After: CO = 0, O₂ = 0.150 − 0.100 = 0.050 mol, CO₂ = 0.200 mol.
P(O₂) = 0.050 × 16.41 = 0.82 atm; P(CO) = 0; P(CO₂) = 0.200 × 16.41 = 3.28 atm; P_tot = 4.10 atm.
The pressure dropped because 3 mol of gas become 2 mol of gas (fewer particles in the same V and T). Method: do the limiting-reactant bookkeeping in moles first, then convert each gas to a pressure.
