Problem 1 (15 points, 10 min):

The dipole moments and bond lengths of two gas-phase hydrogen halides are: HF, μ = 1.826 D, R = 91.7 pm; HBr, μ = 0.827 D, R = 141.4 pm. (a) Calculate the %-ionic character of each bond. (b) Use the electronegativity table to classify each bond as covalent, polar covalent, or ionic according to the ΔEN rule. Does your result from (a) support the classification of HF? Comment. (c) Draw both molecules with their dipole moment vectors in the convention used in this course.

Problem 2 (20 points, 10 min):

The cyanate ion, OCN− (C is the central atom), is a stable anion found in many salts, while its isomer, the fulminate ion, CNO− (N is the central atom), forms shock-sensitive explosive salts such as mercury(II) fulminate. (a) Draw all reasonable Lewis resonance structures of both ions and assign formal charges to every atom. (b) Identify the most important resonance structure of each ion and use formal-charge arguments to explain which isomer should be more stable. (c) Estimate the C–N and C–O bond orders in the cyanate ion by averaging over its two most important resonance structures. (d) Give the shape and the hybridization of the central atom in the cyanate ion.

Problem 3 (10 points, 5 min):

(a) Rank the O–N–O bond angles of NO2+, NO2•, and NO2− from largest to smallest and explain your ranking in terms of the space requirements of the electron density regions on N. (b) In SF4, explain why the lone pair occupies an equatorial and not an axial position, and why the axial F–S–F angle is 173° rather than 180°.

Problem 4 (10 points, 5 min):

Phosphorus pentachloride, PCl5, is trigonal bipyramidal. Its equatorial P–Cl bonds are 202 pm long while its axial bonds are 214 pm. (a) Give the hybridization of P and state which atomic orbitals of P are used for the equatorial bonds and which for the axial bonds. (b) Explain why the axial bonds are longer. (c) Explain why NCl5 does not exist even though N is in the same group as P.

Problem 5 (20 points, 10 min):

(a) Use MO theory to explain why He2 does not exist but the He2+• cation can be observed in the gas phase. Give both bond orders. (b) Experimentally, B2 is paramagnetic. Write the electron configuration of B2 using both possible orderings of the σg2p and πu2p MOs and use the result to decide which ordering is correct for B2. What effect is responsible for this ordering? (c) Potassium carbide, K2C2, contains the C22− anion. Give the bond order and magnetism of C2 and of C22−, and rank the C–C bond lengths in C2, C22−, and ethane, C2H6. (d) What is the total spin S of ground-state O2?

Problem 6 (10 points, 5 min):

A 2.500-g sample of a magnesium sulfate hydrate, MgSO4·xH2O, is heated until all water of hydration has been driven off. The anhydrous MgSO4 left behind has a mass of 1.221 g. Determine x and the mass-% of water in the hydrate.

Problem 7 (20 points, 10 min):

A 0.2000-g sample of a fragrant liquid that contains only C, H, and O is burned completely in excess O2. The combustion yields 0.3996 g CO2 and 0.1636 g H2O. In a separate experiment, the vapor of the compound has a density of 2.487 g/L at 150. °C and 0.980 atm. (a) Determine the masses of C, H, and O in the sample. (b) Determine the empirical formula. (c) Determine the molar mass from the vapor density and give the molecular formula. (d) Write the balanced combustion equation.

Problem 8 (15 points, 10 min):

Concentrated nitric acid is 70.0% HNO3 by mass and has a density of 1.413 g/mL. (a) Calculate its molarity, its molality, and the mole fraction of HNO3. (b) What volume of the concentrated acid is needed to make 500. mL of 1.50 M HNO3(aq)? (c) Name one safety rule for this dilution.

Problem 9 (10 points, 5 min):

200. mL of physiological saline solution (0.154 M NaCl) and 300. mL of 0.100 M CaCl2(aq) are combined in a 1-L volumetric flask, and the flask is filled to the mark with de-ionized water. (a) Give the concentrations of Na+, Ca2+, and Cl− and the total ion concentration in the final solution. (b) What mass of AgCl(s) (143.32 g/mol) would precipitate if excess AgNO3 were added to the whole solution?

Problem 10 (20 points, 10 min):

An antacid tablet containing 0.840 g sodium bicarbonate, NaHCO3 (84.007 g/mol), is dissolved in water and reacts with 25.0 mL of 0.300 M HCl(aq), about the acid in your stomach. (a) Take qualitative chemical inventory of the two solutions before mixing. (b) What types of reaction occur? Write the net ionic equation(s) and identify the conjugate acid/base pairs. (c) Why is HCO3− called amphoteric? Give its conjugate acid and conjugate base. (d) Determine the limiting reactant and the volume of CO2(g) released at body temperature (37 °C) and 1.00 atm. (e) HCl is a strong acid but HF is a weak acid, even though F is more electronegative than Cl. Explain.

Problem 11 (15 points, 5 min):

Ethanol burns according to C2H5OH(l) + 3 O2(g) ⟶ 2 CO2(g) + 3 H2O(g). (a) Using the Lewis structure of ethanol and the allocation of bonding electrons by electronegativity, assign the oxidation number of each of the two carbon atoms in ethanol. (b) Give the oxidation numbers of all atoms in O2, CO2, and H2O. (c) Identify the oxidizing and the reducing agent and determine the total number of electrons transferred per molecule of ethanol. (d) Explain why oxidation numbers should not be taken as real charges.

Problem 12 (20 points, 10 min):

A 0.200-g strip of magnesium is dropped into 25.0 mL of 0.800 M HCl(aq). (a) Write the balanced molecular and net ionic equations. (b) Determine the limiting reactant. (c) What volume of dry H2(g) forms at 25 °C and 755 mm-Hg? (d) After the reaction stops, the remaining acid is titrated with 0.250 M NaOH(aq). What volume of NaOH is needed to reach the equivalence point? (This is called a back-titration.)

Problem 13 (15 points, 10 min):

Uranium for nuclear reactors is enriched by effusion of gaseous UF6. (a) Calculate the ratio of effusion rates of 235UF6 and 238UF6 (atomic masses: 235U 235.044 Da, 238U 238.051 Da, F 18.998 Da). (b) Natural uranium contains 0.720% 235U. Each effusion stage multiplies the 235U/238U ratio by the factor from (a). How many stages are needed to reach 3.00% 235U? (c) Calculate the root-mean-square speed of 238UF6 molecules at 60. °C.

Problem 14 (15 points, 10 min):

A sample of natural gas contains only methane, CH4, and ethane, C2H6. Its density is 0.818 g/L at 25.0 °C and 1.00 atm. (a) Determine the average molar mass of the mixture. (b) Determine the mole fraction of each component. (c) Give the partial pressure of each gas. (d) Which component would deviate more from ideal gas behavior and why?
