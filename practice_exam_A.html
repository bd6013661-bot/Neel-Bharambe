Problem 1 (15 points, 10 min):

Draw Lewis diagrams, using the Natta projection (line-dash-wedge notation) if necessary, and predict the correct molecular shape of the following species based on VSEPR theory. Tell whether these compounds are polar or non-polar. If polar, draw the molecular dipole moment vector using the IUPAC convention.

(a) Chlorine trifluoride, ClF3.   (b) Xenon difluoride, XeF2.   (c) Phosphorus trichloride, PCl3.

Problem 2 (20 points, 10 min):

Describe the molecular bonds in the ethene molecule, aka ethylene, C2H4, using concepts from valence bond theory. For full credit: write the correct Lewis structure, give the local geometry, bond angles, and hybridization of the two carbon atoms. Sketch the principal axes (x, y, and z) of a 3D coordinate system next to your structure. For each bond in C2H4, indicate whether it is a σ- or a π-bond and which atomic and/or hybrid orbitals overlap to form it. Sketch the bond orbital(s) between the two carbon atoms. Give the total number of σ- and π-bonds in the molecule.

Problem 3 (20 points, 10 min):

Consider the nitrosonium cation, NO+, the nitric oxide radical, NO•, and the nitroxyl anion, NO−. Based on the molecular orbital diagram for 2nd row diatomic molecules (use the ordering valid for N2), write the valence electron configuration of each species, give its bond order, tell whether it is para- or diamagnetic, and assign the correct bond length to each species. The bond lengths in the gas phase are 106.3 pm, 115.1 pm, and 125.8 pm. In which atomic orbitals does the electron density of the bonding MOs predominantly reside, N or O? Why?

Problem 4 (15 points, 10 min):

Methyl isocyanate, CH3NCO, is a highly toxic industrial intermediate (Bhopal disaster, 1984). The atoms are connected in the order H3C–N–C–O. Draw the correct Lewis structure including all formal charges that are not zero, give the hybridization of each C, N, and O atom, and estimate the C–N–C and N–C–O bond angles. Determine the molar mass. Show work for full credit.

Problem 5 (10 points, 5 min):

The black magnetic mineral magnetite is an iron oxide with the experimental %-composition 72.36% Fe and 27.64% O by mass. Determine its empirical formula. Show your work for full credit.

Problem 6 (15 points, 5 min):

Caffeine has the %-composition 49.48% C, 5.19% H, 28.85% N, and 16.48% O by mass. Mass spectrometry gives a molar mass of about 194 g/mol. Determine the empirical and the molecular formula of caffeine. Show your work for full credit.

Problem 7 (20 points, 10 min):

Iron is produced in a blast furnace by the reduction of iron(III) oxide with carbon monoxide according to the (unbalanced) equation:

Fe2O3(s) + CO(g) ⟶ Fe(l) + CO2(g)

(a) Correctly balance this rxn equation using the smallest whole numbers as coefficients.
(b) 50.0 g Fe2O3 is reacted with 25.0 g CO. Determine the limiting reactant.
(c) Determine the theoretical yield of Fe and the mass of excess reactant left over.
(d) 28.9 g Fe was actually isolated. Determine the %-yield of the rxn.

Potentially helpful information: molar weights in g/mol: 159.687 (Fe2O3); 28.010 (CO); 55.845 (Fe); 44.009 (CO2).

Problem 8 (10 points, 5 min):

Nigel needs 250. mL of 0.0750 M HCl(aq) for a titration. The stockroom only has 6.00 M HCl(aq). Calculate the volume of stock solution he needs and describe in two or three sentences how he should prepare the solution (name the glassware).

Problem 9 (15 points, 15 min):

Explain in words how to make a 0.2000 molal aqueous solution of copper(II) sulfate that contains a total of 500.0 g of water as solvent, using unlimited de-ionized water and a bottle of CuSO4·5H2O (copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate, “blue vitriol”). Be quantitative in your description. In one sentence, explain why molality is a more precise concentration measure than molarity.

Problem 10 (15 points, 10 min):

Describe the reaction that takes place when 100. mL of a 0.0200 M aqueous solution of nickel(II) chloride is mixed with 100. mL of a 0.0300 M aqueous solution of sodium hydroxide. For full credit (a) take qualitative chemical inventory of the two reactant solutions; (b) tell what type of reaction takes place; (c) write out the net ionic equation and name the spectator ions; (d) determine the limiting reactant and the mass of solid formed (Ni(OH)2: 92.707 g/mol).

Problem 11 (15 points, 5 min):

A 25.00-mL sample of sulfuric acid of unknown concentration is titrated with 0.1500 M NaOH(aq). The phenolphthalein end point is reached after 31.42 mL of titrant has been added. Write the balanced molecular equation of the neutralization and determine the molarity of the sulfuric acid. What is the difference between the equivalence point and the end point?

Problem 12 (15 points, 10 min):

Zinc metal reacts with excess hydrochloric acid to give hydrogen gas: Zn(s) + 2 HCl(aq) ⟶ ZnCl2(aq) + H2(g). The H2 is collected over water at 25.0 °C. The volume of collected gas is 245 mL and the total pressure is 755 mm-Hg. What mass of zinc reacted?

Potentially helpful information: vapor pressure of water at 25.0 °C = 23.8 mm-Hg; molar mass of Zn = 65.38 g/mol. Assume ideal gas behavior.

(d) Draw its Lewis structure and give its shape.

Assign oxidation numbers to every element in all reactants and products. Identify the element that is oxidized, the element that is reduced, the oxidizing agent, and the reducing agent.
