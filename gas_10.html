Gas Problems: 10-Problem Set

Work on scratch paper or in the spaces. Answers and full worked solutions are in gas_10_key.pdf. ★ = exam-level problem. Use R = 0.08206 L·atm/(mol·K) unless told otherwise.

THE 5-STEP GAS ROUTINE (do this for EVERY gas problem)
  1. Make a table of P, V, n, T. Write each given value with its unit and put "?" for the one you want. Two states (before/after)? Make two columns.
  2. Convert: T → K (+273.15) · P → atm (÷760 for mmHg/Torr, ÷101.325 for kPa) · V → L (mL ÷ 1000) · grams → mol (÷𝓜).
  3. Pick the equation:
    SituationUse
    One state, find one of P, V, n, TPV = nRT
    Same gas, conditions change (before → after)P₁V₁/(n₁T₁) = P₂V₂/(n₂T₂); cross out what stays constant
    Grams + volume given, find 𝓜 or density𝓜 = mRT/(PV) · d = P𝓜/(RT)
    Mixture of gasesP_tot = ΣP_i · P_i = χ_i·P_tot · P_i = n_iRT/V
    Collected over waterP_gas = P_total − P_H₂O
    Reaction involving a gasgas ⇄ mol via PV = nRT, then mole ratio. Same T and P? volume ratio = mole ratio
    Rates / effusionrate₁/rate₂ = √(𝓜₂/𝓜₁) · time₁/time₂ = √(𝓜₁/𝓜₂)
    Speeds / kinetic energyu_rms = √(3RT/𝓜), R = 8.314, 𝓜 in kg/mol · KE_avg = 3/2 RT (per mol)
  4. Predict first: does the answer go up or down? (e.g. hotter → bigger V; more pressure → smaller V)
  5. Solve, then check units, sig figs, and that the direction matches your prediction.

Problems

1. ★ (Combined) A weather balloon holds 5.00 L of He at 755 mm-Hg and 25 °C at launch. It rises to an altitude where P = 380. mm-Hg and T = −15 °C. What is its volume there? Explain why it expanded even though it got colder.

2. 4.50 g of CO₂ is placed in a 2.00-L flask at body temperature (37 °C). What is the pressure in atm?

3. ★ A hydrocarbon contains 85.6% C and 14.4% H by mass. A 0.802-g sample of its vapor occupies 354 mL at 100. °C and 752 mm-Hg. Determine the molar mass and the molecular formula.

4. ★ A 5.00-L flask at 25 °C contains 2.00 g He and 8.00 g O₂. Find the partial pressure of each gas, the total pressure, and the mole fraction of He.

5. ★ Heating KClO₃ gives O₂: 2 KClO₃(s) → 2 KCl(s) + 3 O₂(g). The O₂ is collected over water: 0.512 L at 22 °C and a total pressure of 758 mm-Hg (vapor pressure of water at 22 °C = 19.8 mm-Hg). What mass of KClO₃ (122.55 g/mol) decomposed?

6. ★ Car airbags inflate by 2 NaN₃(s) → 2 Na(s) + 3 N₂(g). What mass of NaN₃ (65.01 g/mol) is needed to fill a 65.0-L airbag at 1.10 atm and 25 °C?

7. 15.0 L H₂ and 6.00 L N₂ react at the same T and P: N₂(g) + 3 H₂(g) → 2 NH₃(g). Which is limiting? What volume of NH₃ forms, and what volume of which gas is left over? (No ideal gas law needed!)

8. ★ 2.00 g of Al (26.982 g/mol) is reacted with 1.50 L of Cl₂ gas at 25 °C and 1.00 atm: 2 Al(s) + 3 Cl₂(g) → 2 AlCl₃(s). Determine the limiting reactant and the mass of AlCl₃ (133.33 g/mol) formed.

9. (a) How many times faster does H₂ effuse than O₂? (b) An unknown gas effuses 0.354 times as fast as He. What is its molar mass, and what could it be? (c) 1.00 L of N₂ effuses through a pinhole in 105 s; the same volume of an unknown gas takes 191 s. Find the unknown's molar mass.

10. (a) Calculate u_rms for He and for Xe (131.29 g/mol) at 25 °C. (b) At what temperature would O₂ molecules have the same u_rms as He atoms at 25 °C?
